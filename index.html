Case Study // Biomechanical Additive Manufacturing & DIC

Biomimetic 3D-Printed Heart Simulator

Fig 1: DIC Speckle Pattern & Port Clamps

High-contrast surface dot distribution prepared for Ncorr strain tracking.

Fig 2: Closed Hydraulic Test Loop

Diaphragm pump, inline flow meter, relief valve, and check-valve assembly.

The Challenge: Develop a compliant, 3D-printed cardiac model capable of repeatable expansion and contraction under cyclic hydraulic pressure without experiencing layer-line delamination, fatigue creep, or seam leakage at rigid-to-soft tubing interfaces.

Technical Execution & Daniel's Contributions:

  • Fluid System Review & Working Fluid Staging: Sized a closed hydraulic loop converting continuous diaphragm pump flow into a pulsatile waveform using dual inline check valves (0.5 PSI and 2.24 PSI cracking thresholds) and an inline 50 PSI bronze safety relief valve. Staged fluids from water to a blood-analog 40% glycerol / 60% water mixture (≈3.5–4.5 cP).
  • Theoretical Expansion Modeling: Compared linear thin-walled pressure vessel models against non-linear neo-Hookean hyper-elastic equations ($P_{nonlinear} \approx \frac{0.404tE}{r}$), predicting ≈46–47 psi required to achieve 20% radial strain on the TPU shell.
  • Digital Image Correlation (DIC) Strain Validation: Speckled the elastomeric outer surface and performed non-contact full-field 2D strain analysis in MATLAB using the Ncorr DIC engine.
  • Quantitative Results: Verified peak in-plane normal strains of $E_{xx} = 5.12\%$ and $E_{yy} = 5.14\%$ with a peak horizontal displacement of 0.1309 in and vertical displacement of -0.2858 in, proving non-rigid, true elastic compliance during dynamic pump cycling.
  • Manufacturing Architecture: Authored recommendations to transition from direct FDM TPU printing to a sacrificial 3D-printed mold for homogeneous silicone casting to eliminate anisotropic layer-line failure points.
Key Metrics: 5.14% Peak Strain (Ncorr DIC) • SpiderFlex TPU 75A • 19.8mm Standardized Ports • 130 PSI Loop
